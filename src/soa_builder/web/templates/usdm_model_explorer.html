Welcome to the USDM Model Explorer

The CDISC Unified Study Definitions Model (USDM) describes everything in a clinical study protocol — its design, schedule, objectives, eligibility rules and more — as structured, machine-readable data. This explorer lets you walk through the whole model, v4.0.0, entirely offline.

Five ideas to get you started

Entity — a kind of thing the model can describe, like a Study, an Encounter (a visit) or an Objective. Each entity is one page in this explorer.
Attribute — a named piece of information an entity holds, such as an Encounter's type or a Study's name.
Cardinality — how many values an attribute may hold: 1 means exactly one, 0..1 means optional, 0..* means any number.
Contains vs. references — a contains attribute owns its data (the data lives inside, like arms inside a design); a references attribute just points at something defined elsewhere, by its id.
Abstract entity — a template that is never used directly. Real data always uses one of its subtypes: for example, StudyDesign is abstract, and actual studies use InterventionalStudyDesign or ObservationalStudyDesign.

Glossary

NCI C-code
A permanent identifier (like C15320) from the NCI Thesaurus, giving every entity, attribute and term an unambiguous meaning.
Preferred term
The official human-readable name registered for a C-code.
Code list / permitted values
For some attributes, USDM fixes the allowed answers as a published list of coded terms — expand them in any attribute table.
Worked example (LZZT)
A complete real protocol — the CDISC Pilot Study of LY246708 in Alzheimer's disease — expressed in USDM. Turn on “Worked example” to see how each entity is actually used.
Inheritance
Some entities extend a parent and take over its attributes; inherited attributes are marked with their origin.
Schedule of activities
The plan of who does what, when: timelines of scheduled activity instances tied to encounters and timings.
